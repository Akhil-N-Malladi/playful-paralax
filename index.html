Probability / lost boarding pass paradox

One wrong seat. Ninety-nine decisions later.

There are 100 passengers and 100 numbered seats. Passenger 1 loses their boarding pass and picks a seat at random.

Each later passenger takes their own seat if it is free. Otherwise, they pick a random empty seat. What is the chance Passenger 100 gets Seat 100?

Choose the answer that feels right
01lost pass
01 02 ··· 99 100
random seat → displaced passenger → random seat → …

After all that randomness, what would you guess for Passenger 100?

Pick the answer that seems most intuitive.

Why this happens

The random chain ends when it reaches Seat 1 or Seat 100. Those two seats play symmetric roles, so each is equally likely to appear first.

What happens inside the model?

Each path draws a standard normal shock each day. Geometric Brownian motion updates the price with Δt = 1/252, so 252 trading days represent one year.

Drift and volatility stay constant. The demo omits regimes, jumps, fees, and fundamentals. It shows uncertainty, not a return forecast.
